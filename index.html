FIELD NOTES / 01

A farm is a system.

This concept imagines a pressurized, climate-controlled greenhouse connected to a habitat. The growing medium is assumed to have already been treated for perchlorates and amended with nutrients; untreated Martian regolith is not ready for planting.

Recycled water, root-zone irrigation, supplemental lighting, power, and life support are essential dependencies. These are conceptual assumptions from the supplied research, not a validated Mars farming plan.

Digging is 5× faster than an assumed Mars work pace of 15 seconds per plot. This baseline is illustrative, not a measured or validated estimate of actual Martian digging performance. In the demo, each of the 40 plots takes 3 seconds, or about 2 minutes of active digging in total.

Watered potatoes reach harvest after 10 minutes of active play; resting at night advances their growth. The five growth stages and day/night cycle are accelerated for demonstration.

The astronaut's stepping motion uses Mars surface gravity (3.71 m/s², about 0.38 Earth gravity). Suit movement is illustrative; the rover, soil and crops are not a full physical simulation.

On touch devices, drag the joystick to move, swipe the view to orbit, tap a plot to target it and hold Work. Open Controls for alternative movement, work, camera and rest buttons: tap a direction again to stop; work stops after each plot. With a keyboard, use WASD or arrows to move, Space to work, 1–3 to change attachments, R to rest, Q/E and I/K to orbit, and +/− to zoom. A standard gamepad uses the left stick or D-pad to move, right stick to orbit, triggers to zoom, A to work, B to rest, bumpers to change tools and Start for these notes.
