### Grow potatoes on Mars.

A tracked machine swaps powered heads to prepare, plant and harvest potatoes in a Martian habitat.

**HOW TO PLAY**  
**SCROLL TO READ ↓**

Move with WASD or the joystick. Hold Space or Work to follow blue markers and work plots; release after each objective. Select the numbered tools as you progress.

1. **Till · Tool 1**  
   Loosen the soil to give the potatoes room to grow.
2. **Furrow · Tool 2**  
   Use the plow to open the planting rows.
3. **Drill · Tool 3**  
   Use the auger to make holes for the seed potatoes.
4. **Plant by hand**  
   Work each plot to take a seed potato from the machine's bin and plant it.
5. **Water**  
   Park the machine with F or Park tool. Walk to the white cabinet and press Space or Work at its switch.
6. **Hill · Tool 2 again**  
   Pick up the machine and select the plow again. Mound soil over the shoots to protect the potatoes from light.
7. **Water again**  
   Park the machine and use the cabinet switch when the growing tubers are ready.
8. **Harvest · Tool 4**  
   Pick up the machine and use the lifter to harvest. Select the tiller to start another crop; your total is kept.

At night, approach the habitat door and press Space or Work to rest and speed up growth.

This concept demonstration uses accelerated time and growth; its environment and farming systems are imagined.

## Menu / Field Notes

### A farm is a system.

This concept imagines a pressurized, climate-controlled greenhouse connected to a habitat. The growing medium is assumed to have already been treated for perchlorates and amended with nutrients; untreated Martian regolith is not ready for planting.

Recycled water, root-zone irrigation, supplemental lighting, power, and life support are essential dependencies. These are conceptual assumptions from the supplied research, not a validated Mars farming plan.

Twenty plots and accelerated work keep this a short experience. The powered heads till, furrow and hill, bore planting holes, and lift the harvest; the sealed caddy protects their couplers from dust. Seed potatoes are stored beneath the caddy and planted by hand.

Park the machine and use the switch on the white cabinet to run the sprinklers for five seconds after planting and again during tuber growth. Automatic watering between these two checkpoints represents recurring irrigation, not just two waterings for the season. Growth takes 30 seconds per plot, paused at checkpoints until the work is done; night rest advances it by up to 15 seconds.

Progress saves automatically in this browser on this device. It does not sync between devices and may be removed if site data is cleared. The mission panel warns you if saving is unavailable.

Button: **RESET PROGRESS**

On Earth, potatoes need consistent moisture, especially during tuber development. The [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-potatoes) recommends roughly an inch of water per week and watering once or twice weekly, more often in sandy soil. A Mars habitat would need moisture sensing and site-specific irrigation; this demo does not model water quantities or prescribe an operating schedule.

The astronaut's walking animation is illustrative, not a physical simulation of movement in Mars gravity. The rover, soil and crops are likewise simplified.

Hold Work to approach and service eligible plots in sequence; release and press again after an objective completes. Use F, the Park Tool button, or gamepad B to leave the machine; return to its handle to pick it up. At the white cabinet switch, press Space, Work, or gamepad A to start a timed irrigation cycle. At night, approach the glowing habitat door and press Space, Work, or gamepad A to rest. On touch devices, drag the joystick to move, swipe the view to orbit, tap a plot to target it and hold Work. Tap Tools to open the attachment menu. Open Controls for alternative movement, work, and camera buttons. With a keyboard, use WASD to move, arrows to pan the camera, C to recenter, 1–4 to change attachments, Q to orbit clockwise, E to orbit counterclockwise, I to look up, K to look down, and +/− to zoom. Pinch or scroll on a trackpad, or use the mouse wheel to zoom. A standard gamepad uses the left stick or D-pad to move, right stick to orbit, triggers to zoom, bumpers to change tools and Start for these notes. Hover, focus or tap an attachment for its purpose.

## Operation infographic

Header: **FIELD GUIDE**

| Title | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Rotary tiller · loosen | Curved tines break up compacted soil so roots and tubers have room to grow. |
| Hilling plow · furrow | The plow opens a trench, giving seed potatoes a consistent planting row. |
| Mixing auger · bore | The spiral drills planting holes with little downward force in low gravity. |
| Planting by hand | Placing each seed potato gently in a prepared hole helps protect it from damage. |
| Sprinklers · establish | Water moistens the planted soil, helping the seed potatoes begin growing. |
| Hilling plow · mound | Soil mounded over growing tubers protects them from light and greening. |
| Sprinklers · tuber growth | Consistent moisture during tuber development helps the crop grow evenly. |
| Vibratory lifter · harvest | The blade lifts from beneath while soil sifts away, reducing damage to potatoes. |
