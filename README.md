# Fieldwork 01: Martian Potatoes

A static 3D concept demo for a tracked Martian agriculture multi-tool. No account, server-side code, or build step is required.

## Run and publish

Serve this folder with any static web server (ES modules need HTTP, not `file://`). For local development, run `npm install` and `npm run serve`, then open `http://localhost:8080`. Publish the folder to a GitHub repository and enable GitHub Pages from the repository's root branch. All game assets, including Three.js, are local and paths are relative, so it also works under a GitHub Pages project subpath.

Browser and home-screen icons use the supplied emblem. Social previews use the PNG card in `assets/social-preview.png`; the absolute Open Graph and Twitter URLs in `index.html` target the domain in `CNAME` and should be updated if the site moves. Sharing services may cache previews.

To preview on a phone on the same Wi-Fi, start the server on all network interfaces (for example `npx http-server . -a 0.0.0.0 -p 8081 -c-1`) and open `http://<computer-local-ip>:8081` on the phone. The computer's firewall and Wi-Fi network must allow the connection. This exposes the project folder to devices on that network; stop the server when finished.

## Controls

- Background music loops automatically when allowed by the browser; otherwise it starts on the first interaction. The app's copy has its quiet ending trimmed to avoid a pause between loops. Use **Music** on the welcome screen or the music button in the top bar to mute or resume it.
- Sound effects accompany the tiller (its roller spins and its sound loops while Work is held with the tiller mounted and plots remain to be tilled), plow, auger, lifter, irrigation, tool attachment and seed pickup. They are mixed below the background music. Use **Sound effects** in Field Notes to mute them independently of the music for the current session. Audio starts after browser interaction.
- The temporary **Sound mixer** in Field Notes provides individual sliders and looping previews. Values are percentages of each original recording. Levels save on this device independently of mission progress and survive a progress reset; clearing browser site data removes them.
- WASD: move the astronaut and tracked tool relative to the camera. W moves away from the camera; A / D move left / right in the view.
- Arrow keys pan the camera across the ground; `C` recenters it on the astronaut.
- Press `Escape` to open or close the Field Notes menu and pause the experience.
- Supported phones and controllers provide optional haptic feedback for movement, attachments, fieldwork and irrigation. Turn it off in Field Notes for the current session. Vibration and controller rumble depend on browser, device and connection support; iOS browsers do not expose the standard web vibration API.
- Right-drag to orbit; Q orbits clockwise, E counterclockwise, I looks up (including into the sky), and K looks down. Pinch or scroll on a trackpad, use the mouse wheel, or press `+` / `-` to zoom.
- Click an eligible plot to target it from anywhere; otherwise the nearest eligible plot is selected automatically. A floating blue diamond marks the selected plot for every activity, even if the required attachment is not yet selected.
- Click an attachment or press `1`, `2`, `3`, `4` to change it. Hover, focus or tap an attachment to learn its purpose. The astronaut opens the sealed tool caddy and exchanges the rotary tiller, furrowing and hilling plow, mixing auger, or vibratory lifter.
- Press `F`, select **Park tool**, or press gamepad B to leave the machine and walk independently. Return to its handle and use the same control to pick it up.
- While walking independently, the astronaut faces the direction of travel and turns with the camera when standing still.
- Nearby interaction prompts identify the key or control for the machine handle, irrigation switch, habitat door, and workable plots.
- During either irrigation objective, park the machine, walk to the blue marker at the white cabinet, and press Space, Work, or gamepad A at its switch. Sprinklers water the whole field for five seconds and stop automatically; the second cycle becomes available once all plots reach the growth checkpoint.
- Hold Space: approach the highlighted plot, work, then automatically continue through eligible plots in a loose back-and-forth row pattern. The machine turns on its tracks before driving forward, with the astronaut following behind. Releasing Space stops the approach or work and resets that plot's work. Release and press again after an objective completes. Follow the objective panel for the required attachment and activity.
- A field-guide infographic appears during each operation to explain why that tool or action is needed at that stage.
- Once the harvest is complete, select the rotary tiller to prepare the next crop. The field begins a new cycle while time, position and the cumulative harvest are preserved. The distant transparent silo fills as potatoes are harvested and retains its contents between cycles and reloads.
- In the browser developer console, `setSiloPreview(100)` temporarily shows a chosen potato count without changing saved progress; `setSiloPreview(null)` restores the actual harvest. The count must be a non-negative safe integer. Individual potatoes rain into a mixed pile up to the visible silo's rendered height; totals can continue growing beyond it. Large additions arrive gradually to keep the animation responsive. The translucent circle in the bottom-left corner hides or restores the interface.
- At night, approach the glowing habitat door and press Space or Work to rest. A five-second habitat sequence advances crop growth by up to 15 seconds, subject to the next required checkpoint.
- On a small or touch device, drag the joystick to move, swipe the 3D view to orbit, pinch the view to zoom, tap a plot to target it and hold Work. Tap **Tools** to open or close the attachment menu; tap an attachment to switch and read its description. Tap Work at the door to rest. Release the joystick to stop moving and release Work to stop working.
- Select **Controls** for alternative on-screen buttons. Tap a direction to move and tap it again to stop; pressing a WASD key also stops the on-screen direction and gives control to the keyboard. Tap Work to start or stop the automatic sequence, or to rest at the door. Work stops automatically after an objective is complete. The panel also provides camera and zoom buttons. Selecting an attachment stops on-screen movement and work.
- Standard-mapped gamepad: left stick or D-pad moves, right stick orbits and tilts, triggers zoom, hold A / bottom button to work or press it at the door to rest, and bumpers change attachments. A starts the experience; Start opens or closes field notes. Browser and controller support varies; some browsers require a button press before the controller is detected.

There are 20 plots. Use tool 1 to till, tool 2 to furrow, and tool 3 to drill; plant by hand and start the sprinklers. Select tool 2 again to hill soil over the growing shoots, then run the second irrigation cycle during tuber development and harvest with tool 4. The mission panel names the attachment needed next; the required tool and mobile tool drawer glow and pulse until the correct attachment is selected (with a static highlight for reduced-motion preferences). The panel also shows objective progress, watering progress, and the cumulative number of potatoes harvested. In this simplified demo each harvested plot counts as one potato, rather than estimating a real crop yield. The astronaut takes seed potatoes from the bin beneath the tool caddy and plants them by hand. Planting takes 3 seconds per plot; other field activities take 0.8–1.2 seconds per plot, plus automatic approach, travel and tool changes. Each field-wide sprinkler cycle takes five seconds. After irrigation starts, crops grow through five stages over 30 seconds per plot, pausing at the hilling and irrigation checkpoints until serviced. The simulation pauses while the field notes are open or the browser is not rendering.

The two interactive sprinkler cycles represent initial watering and a mid-growth check; additional irrigation is assumed to continue between them and afterward. They are not the only two waterings of a real crop. [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-potatoes) advises consistent moisture, roughly an inch of water per week, and watering once or twice weekly, more often in sandy soils. The demo does not simulate water quantities or prescribe an irrigation schedule for Mars; a real habitat would need moisture sensing and site-specific calibration.

## Concept and limitations

Time, plant growth, and the day/night cycle are accelerated and simplified. The astronaut's distance-based walking animation is illustrative, not a physical simulation of movement in Mars gravity. The demo does not fully simulate rover traction, soil, plants, or human biomechanics. It assumes treated, nutrient-amended growing medium, a pressurized and climate-controlled dome, recycled water, root-zone irrigation, supplemental lighting, power, and life support. These are conceptual assumptions, not an operational procedure or a validated model of Martian agriculture. See the included research PDF for the project's system analysis and its stated assumptions.

The clock maps one sol (about 24 hours, 39 minutes, 35 seconds on Earth) to four minutes of active play. The sky approximates an equinox at 18.44° N, with the Sun rising around 06:00 and setting around 18:00; the separate 19:00–06:00 rest window is unchanged. Phobos and Deimos use their approximate orbital periods of 7 hours 39.2 minutes and 30 hours 17.9 minutes: Phobos rises in the west, while Deimos rises in the east. Circular equatorial orbits and arbitrary starting positions are used, so this is not a real-date ephemeris and does not model seasons, orbital inclination or eclipses. Deimos is slightly enlarged for visibility. Sources: [NASA on the moons' orbits](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/crism-views-phobos-and-deimos/) and [Royal Museums Greenwich on the Martian sol](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/how-long-day-on-mars). The suspended grow lights are illustrative, not a calibrated horticultural lighting system.

The 3D view requires WebGL and a reasonably capable device; performance and controller support vary by browser and hardware. Progress, crop growth, time, machine and parked astronaut positions, the selected attachment, and an active sprinkler countdown save automatically in the current browser on this device. Partially completed plot work and an in-progress tool exchange restart on reload. Saves do not sync across browsers or devices and may be deleted when site data is cleared. The mission panel reports storage errors; an invalid save is not overwritten.

To delete the saved mission and begin from scratch, choose **Reset progress** on the welcome screen or in Field Notes, check the confirmation box, and select **Delete progress**. This removes only this demo's saved progress in the current browser, including the cumulative harvest; it cannot be undone.

## Development

Run `npm test` for the simulation tests. The checked-in `vendor` files are the Three.js 0.180.0 browser modules and license, copied from the package so the demo runs on GitHub Pages without a build or a CDN.
